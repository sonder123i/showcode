Note: the app interface is Chinese-only. It parses Chinese carrier SMS and notifications,
which is what Chinese pickup stations send.

The pain of picking up a parcel is not "my tracking info is scattered". It is the 30 seconds
in front of the locker: digging through three apps for a code, not knowing which slot,
not knowing how long until the locker starts charging.

ShowCode is not a parcel archive. It is the panel you use while standing at the locker.

Automatic capture
· Parses pickup codes, slot / shelf numbers and which station they belong to from SMS and app notifications.
· Backfills the last 14 days of your inbox on install — parcels already on your phone should not
  wait for the next SMS.
· If one message contains several parcels, all of them are captured. The second one does not vanish.

Stations are the first-class object
· The home screen is grouped by station ("how many parcels here, what is the most urgent state"),
  not a time-sorted list of parcels.
· The desktop widget is sectioned the same way; tapping a section goes straight to that station.

Three urgency states
· Safe / due soon / at risk — three colours only. Lockers count down free storage,
  stations count down to closing time. Urgency within "due soon" is carried by a ticking
  countdown, not by adding a fourth colour.

It never guesses on your behalf
· Same station, different spelling: the app does not merge automatically, it only asks.
· Merging shows a preview, is undoable for 5 seconds, keeps a permanent history and is reversible —
  undoing loses nothing.
· When a code cannot be attributed to a station, it goes to "not recognised" for you to confirm,
  instead of inventing a name and producing a card that looks fine.

Privacy
· All parsing happens on the device. No uploads, no accounts, no ads, no analytics SDKs.
· Data lives in a local SQLite database and disappears on uninstall.
· Works without the SMS permission: manual import is a first-class entry point.

Known limits
· Android only. iOS has no API to read SMS bodies or other apps' notifications at all.
· 5G messages, network SMS without a sender number, and collapsed WeChat notifications cannot be
  parsed — but they land in the "not recognised" list rather than being dropped silently.
